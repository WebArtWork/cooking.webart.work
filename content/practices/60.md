## 60. Transaction Cost Economics / Економіка трансакційних витрат

### EN

#### Q1 · single_choice · foundation · 1
What question does transaction cost economics try to answer?
A) How to maximise the share price
B) Why some work is bought on the market while other work is done inside the firm
C) How to set the right price for a new product
D) How to forecast demand for the next year
**Answer:** B
**Why:** The theory explains the boundary between market and firm by comparing the costs of dealing on the market with the costs of managing work internally. Share price, pricing and forecasting are questions for other tools.

#### Q2 · single_choice · foundation · 1
Which of these is a **transaction cost**?
A) The cost of raw materials in a product
B) Wages of factory workers
C) The cost of searching for suppliers, negotiating contracts, monitoring quality and enforcing agreements
D) The depreciation of a machine
**Answer:** C
**Why:** Transaction costs are the costs of making an exchange happen: finding, negotiating, checking and enforcing. Materials, wages and depreciation are production costs, which exist whether work is bought or done in-house.

#### Q3 · true_false · foundation · 1
Asset specificity means that an investment loses much of its value outside one particular relationship.
**Answer:** true
**Why:** A mould that fits only one customer’s product is worth little to anyone else, which leaves the investor exposed. That exposure is what makes high specificity a reason for closer governance.

#### Q4 · true_false · applied · 1
According to the theory, a standard product available from many suppliers is best made inside the firm.
**Answer:** false
**Why:** Many competing suppliers keep prices low and make switching easy, so buying on the market is cheap to run. In-house production fits work that is specific, uncertain and frequent.

#### Q5 · single_choice · applied · 1
Which activity is most likely to be bought through ordinary market deals?
A) Standard office paper
B) A mould used only for one product
C) A proprietary algorithm at the core of the product
D) Training staff in the firm’s own methods
**Answer:** A
**Why:** Paper is a standard product with many sellers, so a deal is simple and easy to check. The others involve assets or knowledge tailored to one firm, where market deals invite hold-up.

#### Q6 · multiple_select · applied · 2
Which **two** conditions push a car maker towards a long-term contract or in-house production of a component?
A) Many suppliers offer the same standard part.
B) The part needs tooling that fits only this buyer.
C) The purchase happens once.
D) Quality is easy to check on delivery.
E) Orders recur every week.
**Answer:** B, E
**Why:** Specific tooling creates dependence, and frequent orders justify the cost of special governance. Many sellers, a one-off purchase and easy checking all favour simple market buying.

#### Q7 · scenario_choice · applied · 1
A printer buys a $400,000 press built only for one publisher’s book format. What is the main risk?
A) Paper prices may rise
B) The machine may run too slowly
C) Hold-up: once installed, the publisher can push prices down, because the press has little value elsewhere
D) Other printers may copy the format
**Answer:** C
**Why:** The press is a specific asset, so the printer cannot easily walk away and the publisher knows it. Paper prices and speed are ordinary business risks, and copying the format is not a problem because the format belongs to the publisher.

#### Q8 · scenario_choice · advanced · 2
An electric-bike maker sells 6,000 bikes a year and orders battery packs weekly. A supplier quotes $310 per pack but needs $250,000 of moulds that fit only this bike, and the specification changes often. In-house production would cost about $330 per pack. What is the best governance?
A) Spot-market quotes each week from the cheapest supplier
B) Buy in-house because that is always the safest
C) Buy in-house because $310 hides costs that make the market always dearer
D) A multi-year contract with shared mould ownership and a price formula
**Answer:** D
**Why:** High specificity, uncertainty and frequency call for protection against hold-up, but in-house production costs $20 more per pack. A hybrid contract protects both sides at lower cost. Spot quotes would expose both to hold-up, and the market is not always dearer.

#### Q9 · multiple_select · advanced · 2
A restaurant chain buys accounting services from an outside firm but employs its own chefs. Which **two** statements explain this using the theory?
A) Accounting is a standard, infrequent service that is easy to buy and check.
B) Chef work needs daily direction and would be costly to contract shift by shift.
C) Accounting involves highly specific assets.
D) Chef work is simple and needs no supervision.
E) Outsourcing is always cheaper than employment.
**Answer:** A, B
**Why:** Accounting is easy to specify and verify, so the market works well, while chef work is continuous and needs direction, so contracting every shift would cost more than employing. Specific assets, simplicity and a rule that outsourcing is always cheaper do not match the facts.

#### Q10 · scenario_choice · advanced · 1
Why do car makers usually buy standard tyres on the open market but design and build their engines themselves?
A) Tyres are cheap, and engines are expensive
B) Tyres are standard products with many suppliers, while engines involve specific designs and frequent changes that would invite hold-up by an outside supplier
C) Engine makers refuse to sell to car makers
D) Car makers have no skills in buying tyres
**Answer:** B
**Why:** The difference lies in specificity, uncertainty and frequency, not in price level. Tyre deals are cheap to run, while engine deals need constant renegotiation and expose the buyer to a supplier’s leverage.

#### Q11 · short_answer · applied · 4
A small hotel buys linen cleaning from a laundry but trains and employs its own receptionists. Use transaction cost economics to explain both choices.
**Sample:** Linen cleaning is standard, many laundries compete and quality is easy to check, so buying it on the market is cheap and no special assets are needed. Reception work needs hotel-specific knowledge, daily direction and constant availability, so contracting it shift by shift would cost more in monitoring and renegotiating than employing staff. The difference lies in specificity, frequency and how easy the work is to check.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Explains why laundry is bought on the market: standard service, many suppliers, easy to check
- 2 | Explains why reception is done in-house: specific knowledge, daily direction, costly to contract

#### Q12 · short_answer · advanced · 4
Explain what hold-up means in transaction cost economics, give an example, and name two ways to protect against it.
**Sample:** Hold-up happens when one side has made an investment tailored to the other, so it cannot leave without a big loss and the other side can raise or cut prices. For example, a printer buys a press that fits only one publisher’s format, and the publisher can then push prices down. Protections include a multi-year contract with minimum volumes, shared investment in the asset, or doing the work in-house.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Explains the mechanism: a specific investment leaves one party dependent and exposed
- 1 | Gives a concrete example
- 1 | Names two protections (long contract with minimum volumes, shared ownership, in-house)

#### Q13 · business_decision · advanced · 5
A coffee chain uses 5 million cups a year. Custom-printed cups cost $0.12 each and need printing plates worth $80,000 that fit only this brand. Standard cups with a printed sleeve cost $0.10 in total. Choose one: custom cups on spot quotes, custom cups on a three-year contract with minimum volumes where the chain owns the plates, or standard cups with sleeves. Justify the choice using the theory and the figures.
**Sample:** I would choose the three-year contract with minimum volumes and chain-owned plates, if the branding is worth more than the $100,000 a year premium. Custom cups cost $600,000 a year against $500,000 for standard cups with sleeves. The plates are specific to us but owned by the chain, so the supplier’s hold-up risk is limited, and orders every week justify a contract rather than spot quotes. Spot quotes would mean renegotiating every time. The risk is that $100,000 a year buys little extra sales, so I would test the brand effect and switch to sleeves if it does not pay.
**Rubric:**
- 1 | Makes one clear choice
- 2 | Applies the theory (specificity, frequency, hold-up) with the figures
- 1 | Weighs the $100,000 premium against the value of branding
- 1 | Names a risk and how to monitor it

#### Q14 · business_decision · expert · 5
A fintech company gets 400,000 support tickets a year. A call centre would charge $6 per ticket, or $2.4 million. In-house support would cost $7.50 per ticket, or $3.0 million. Outsourcing would also need a vendor-management team costing $450,000 a year, and quality failures are estimated to cost $300,000 a year. The tickets need product knowledge and change with every weekly release. Decide how to organise support.
**Sample:** The full cost of outsourcing is $2.4 million + $450,000 + $300,000 = $3.15 million against $3.0 million in-house, so the cheaper unit price is misleading. Knowledge specific to the product, weekly changes and constant volume are all high, which points to in-house. A hybrid may be better: keep complex tickets in-house and outsource a standard first tier with clear scripts. The risk is that the vendor’s quality is hard to monitor, so track resolution rates and review the split every six months.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Calculates the full cost of both options correctly ($3.15 million and $3.0 million)
- 1 | Rates specificity, uncertainty and frequency as high and links them to in-house work
- 1 | Considers a hybrid such as an outsourced standard tier
- 1 | Names a risk or a review point

#### Q15 · case_study · expert · 6
> A furniture maker buys a bespoke fabric from a single supplier, who had to build a $400,000 dyeing line used only for this fabric. The maker orders every week, the designs change each season, and the supplier cannot sell the fabric to anyone else. The options are spot orders, a five-year contract with minimum volumes and shared investment, or buying the supplier for about $1.5 million.
Rate asset specificity, uncertainty and frequency, name the risks on each side, assess the three options and recommend one with specific protections.
**Sample:** Specificity is high because the dyeing line has no other use, uncertainty is high because designs change each season, and frequency is high with weekly orders. The supplier faces hold-up because the maker can push prices down, and the maker faces it because the supplier can raise prices or refuse changes. Spot orders ignore both risks. Buying the supplier removes them but costs $1.5 million and adds management work in a business the maker does not know. I would choose the five-year contract, with minimum volumes that repay the $400,000 line, a shared investment, a price formula, a seasonal process for design changes and agreed exit terms. A review each season would show if the maker should buy later.
**Rubric:**
- 2 | Rates all three dimensions as high with evidence from the case
- 1 | Identifies the risk of hold-up on both sides
- 1 | Assesses all three options (spot orders, contract, acquisition) with their weaknesses
- 2 | Recommends the contract with specific protections (minimum volumes, shared investment, price formula, exit terms) and explains why

**Answer:** C

#### Q4 · true_false · applied · 1

Аутсорсинг також потребував би команди з управління підрядником за $450 000 на рік, а збої якості оцінюють у $300 000 на рік. Звернення потребують знання продукту й змінюються з кожним тижневим релізом. Вирішіть, як організувати підтримку.
**Sample:** Повна вартість аутсорсингу $2,4 млн + $450 000 + $300 000 = $3,15 млн проти $3,0 млн власної, тож нижча ціна за одиницю оманлива. Специфічні знання продукту, щотижневі зміни й постійний обсяг високі, що вказує на власну підтримку. Краще може бути гібрид: складні звернення лишити всередині, а стандартну першу лінію віддати на аутсорс із чіткими скриптами. Ризик у тому, що якість підрядника важко контролювати, тож стежте за рівнем вирішення звернень і переглядайте розподіл кожні півроку.

- 2 | Правильно обчислює повну вартість обох варіантів ($3,15 млн і $3,0 млн)
- 1 | Оцінює специфічність, невизначеність і частоту як високі й пов’язує їх із власною роботою
- 1 | Розглядає гібрид, як-от стандартну лінію на аутсорсі
- 1 | Називає ризик або точку перегляду
